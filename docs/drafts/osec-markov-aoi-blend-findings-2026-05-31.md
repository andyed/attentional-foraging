## Headline finding: Survey is *wide*, not *horizontal*

The prospectus predicted Survey would be H-dominant (the F-pattern's horizontal bar = the H signature of Survey). **It isn't.**

| phase | n_saccades | H̄ mean | V̄ mean | H/V ratio (median) | %H | %V | %diag | %micro | %up-V |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Survey** | 7,903 | 91.5 px | 84.4 px | **0.538** | 30.0% | 24.2% | 35.7% | 10.2% | 32.0% |
| **Evaluate** | 123,409 | 72.2 px | 87.6 px | **0.487** | 27.3% | 28.0% | 28.5% | 16.2% | 37.0% |

The H/V ratio difference between Survey (0.538) and Evaluate (0.487) is small. Survey's dominant direction class is **diagonal** (35.7%), not horizontal (30.0%).

**What this means for the F-pattern story:** The F's horizontal bar is not generated by horizontal Survey *saccades*. It is generated by Survey *fixations clustering in the horizontal top band* — within that band, the saccades between them are diagonal. The horizontal-bar appearance in aggregate heatmaps is a positional artifact of where the fixations land, not a directional artifact of how the eye moves between them.

This is a softening of OSEC's implicit F-decomposition. The Survey-vs-Evaluate distinction stands on amplitude (replicates exactly) and on direction-*entropy* (Survey is more diagonal-mixed, Evaluate is more polarized) — but not on H-dominance.

Categorize: **surprising** per §9 of the prospectus.

## Evaluate is V-dominant — but barely

V̄ (87.6) > H̄ (72.2) during Evaluate, consistent with vertical reading between results. But the H/V ratio median is 0.487 — only marginally V-leaning. The vertical-reading story is true *on average*, with substantial H mass from within-result reading.

The micro-saccade jump from Survey (10.2%) to Evaluate (16.2%) is the cleanest direction signature change: Evaluate has half-again more within-AOI re-fixation than Survey. Reading inside a snippet shows up as micro-saccades; gist sampling does not.

Categorize: **expected, weak**.

## dd_top carousel IS the Survey substrate

The Markov matrix during Survey is dominated by dd_top cells:

- `off → off` (787), `off → dd_top_cell_{1..4}` (793 total) — saccades land in the carousel.
- `dd_top_cell_1 → dd_top_cell_1` (863/1337 = **64.5%** self-loop) — within-cell re-fixation.
- `dd_top_cell_3 → dd_top_cell_3` (594), `→ dd_top_cell_2` (416), `→ dd_top_cell_1` (230) — lateral sampling across the carousel.
- Cross-section to organic during Survey: rare (`dd_top_cell_1 → organic_1` = 60).

**The within-carousel promiscuity finding (`a3ac66ad`) shows up here as Survey-phase lateral cell transitions.** The carousel is the Survey-phase content for this stratum. Whether Survey on `organic_top` SERPs (no widget above) shows similar self-loop concentration on `organic_1` is the second-pass test that decides layout-invariance.

Categorize: **expected** — `a3ac66ad` made the concrete prediction; this is its Markov form.

## Regression substructure — the surprising one

5,738 regressions total (organic_K → organic_K' with K' < K). Subtype counts and where they fire:

| subtype | count | %of regressions | median fix_idx | p25–p75 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| local (\|Δ\|=1–2) | 5,159 | 89.9% | 62 | 38–92 |
| mid (\|Δ\|=3) | 274 | 4.8% | 64 | 46–106 |
| long (\|Δ\|≥4) | 305 | 5.3% | 68 | 46–99 |

### Finding 1: Regressions are 99.6% Evaluate-phase

| phase | local | mid | long |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evaluate | 5,138 | 274 | 304 |
| Survey | 21 | 0 | 1 |

Regressions are a within-Evaluate transition class, not a phase. This validates treating them as a *labeled transition* in the Markov rather than a separate OSEC node. The prospectus's "Regression as a candidate OSEC phase" framing should be retired in favor of "Regression as Evaluate-phase transition subtype."

Categorize: **clarifying**.

### Finding 2: Long regressions do NOT cluster at a recoverable phase point

The Reconsider hypothesis (§6 of the prospectus): if long-organic regressions cluster at a recoverable point in the trial (e.g., always pre-commit after a failed evaluate), they'd be a candidate OSEC node.

The data says no:

- Local median fix_idx: 62
- Mid median: 64
- Long median: 68

These are statistically indistinguishable. Long regressions do not fire later in trials than local regressions. **The Reconsider OSEC-extension is not supported.** Long regressions are a target-driven *destination* class (top-pull) but not a temporal *phase* class.

Categorize: **deflationary** per §9 of the prospectus.

### Finding 3: Long regressions *do* concentrate destinations on the top

Long regression destinations:

| destination | count | %of longs |
|---|---|---|
| organic_2 | 80 | 26.2% |
| organic_1 | 73 | 23.9% |
| organic_3 | 69 | 22.6% |
| organic_4 | 44 | 14.4% |
| organic_5 | 28 | 9.2% |
| organic_6–11 | 11 | 3.6% |

96.4% of long regressions land on organic_1–5. **Long regressions snap to the top half of the page**, never to the bottom. This concretizes `b2be6270` ("long organic regressions are a distinct event class") and `68525e7f` ("long ones have absolute top-pull"). The "absolute top-pull" claim is real: long regressions are not just larger jumps drawn from the same destination distribution as locals, they have a *truncated* destination distribution capped at the top-third.

Compared to local regression destinations (also top-skewed but with a heavier tail — 5.8% reach organic_8 or below):

| | %to organic_1–2 | %to organic_3–5 | %to organic_6+ |
|---|---|---|---|
| local | 42.4% | 39.8% | 17.8% |
| mid | 45.7% | 42.0% | 12.3% |
| long | 50.2% | 46.3% | 3.6% |

The gradient from local→long is real and monotone: longs are not just bigger locals — they have a structurally different destination distribution. The "absolute attractor" claim is grain-correct.

Categorize: **expected** (confirms `68525e7f`).

### Finding 4: Most regressions are adjacent upward steps

The regression-only matrix is dominated by `organic_K → organic_{K-1}`:

| from | to_{K-1} | %of regressions from this row |
|---|---|---|
| organic_2 | organic_1 = 1,076 | 100% |
| organic_3 | organic_2 = 841 / 967 | 87% |
| organic_4 | organic_3 = 749 / 936 | 80% |
| organic_5 | organic_4 = 600 / 821 | 73% |
| organic_6 | organic_5 = 430 / 630 | 68% |
| organic_7 | organic_6 = 340 / 485 | 70% |

The 89.9% local-share of regressions is structural: most "regression" mass is one-step-back re-checking, not multi-step revisiting. This is the active comparison-against-prior-candidate signature in concrete form.

Categorize: **expected**.

## What survived, what didn't

Mapping back to the prospectus §9 surprise predictions:

| Predicted outcome | Resolved |
|---|---|
| Survey is layout-driven | Pending (needs cross-composition pass) |
| Survey's wide saccades are not predominantly horizontal | **CONFIRMED.** Survey is wide and diagonal, not H-dominant. |
| Long-organic regressions cluster at a recoverable phase point (Reconsider candidate) | **FALSIFIED.** No phase-point clustering visible. |
| Regression subtypes are *not* separable by transition-mass distribution | **REJECTED.** The three subtypes have distinguishable destination distributions (Finding 3 gradient). |
| `dd_top` evaluate is not H-dominant | (Not measured at within-carousel grain in this pass — only aggregate. See limitations.) |

So: one prediction confirmed (Survey-not-H), one prediction falsified (Reconsider), and the three-subtype taxonomy holds even though the framing needs to shift from "phase" to "destination class."

---

## What to revise in the prospectus

1. **Retire the "Reconsider" OSEC-extension candidate.** Long regressions are a *destination class*, not a temporal phase. The prospectus §6 framing of long-organic regressions as a candidate phase-point is not supported.
2. **Soften the Survey-as-horizontal-bar story.** The F-pattern's horizontal bar in heatmaps is positional (where fixations cluster), not directional (how saccades move). OSEC's amplitude claim survives; the implicit direction claim does not.
3. **Treat regressions as a transition label, not a phase.** They are 99.6% Evaluate-phase; the Markov substrate handles them as labeled transitions cleanly.

---

## What this run did *not* answer

1. **Layout-invariance of Survey.** Needs `organic_top` and `dd_right_top` strata extracted with the same enrichment pipeline. The cross-composition KL comparison is the headline test and remains open.
2. **Within-carousel H vs V transitions specifically.** Aggregate H/V was computed; per-from/to-subtype was not. The dd_top carousel's lateral-sampling signature should show up as elevated H component on `dd_top_cell_J → dd_top_cell_K'` saccades — a one-line refinement.
3. **LF/HF and pupil signatures at episode grain.** The episode records carry the right entry/exit indices but `mean_pupil` / `mean_lfhf` were not joined in this pass.
4. **Episode-level (vs saccade-level) Markov.** The transition matrices reported are saccade-level. The prospectus framed episodes as the Markov state. Re-running the matrices over episode transitions (collapsing self-loops) is a different and arguably more informative cut.

## Next pass

Three sequential, each ~1 day:

1. **Generalize the stratification.** Build sibling extractors for `organic_top` (no widget above organic) and `dd_right_top`. Recompute phase KL across compositions. Decides layout-invariance.
2. **Episode-level Markov.** Collapse same-state runs and re-estimate transition matrices over episodes (not saccades). Self-loops disappear; only inter-AOI transitions remain. Off-diagonal mass becomes the primary signal.
3. **Within-carousel direction profile.** Restrict the H/V fingerprint to `dd_top_cell_J → dd_top_cell_K'` saccades. Specific test of the `a3ac66ad` within-carousel promiscuity claim as an H-dominant signature.
