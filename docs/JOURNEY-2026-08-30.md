# 2026-08-30 — a day on the AdSERP/AllSERP substrate

Written for whoever picks this up next. It is ordered as it happened, including
the wrong turns, because the wrong turns are most of the value: four of the
day's conclusions were confidently wrong and each was caught the same way.

**Read this first if you are about to "fix" something in the AOI pipeline.**

---

## The one-paragraph version

Sara Al-Lawati reviewed the typed-AOI substrate and reported three defects. Two
are real and now fixed or characterised; one turned out to be a repair mechanism
working correctly. Along the way a genuine, previously-unknown defect surfaced
(mouse telemetry is in a different coordinate space from the AOI boxes) and was
fixed in three places. A fidelity harness was built so future claims are
measurable. The harness then produced a wrong number of its own, which was
caught, and its `aoi` check has been rewritten. Net: the substrate is in better
shape than it was, and — more usefully — it is now *measurable*.

## What is actually true at the end of the day

### Confirmed defects, fixed
1. **Coordinate spaces.** Every trial records `screen 1280`, `document 1403`,
   `window 1422`. Gazepoint (hardware) reports SCREENSHOT space; evtrack
   (JavaScript) reports DOCUMENT space; the typed AOI maps are screenshot space.
   Nothing converted the mouse stream. `get_trial_meta` reads both metadata
   lines with `.split('x')[1]`, keeping the heights and discarding both widths,
   which is why a 123px discrepancy sat unread in 2,776 files.
   - Scale is **anisotropic**: x 0.9123 (= 1280/1403), y **0.9000**. Both are
     needed; y matters more than x.
   - Evidence: click-in-AOI containment 78.0% -> 96.2%; mean cursor-to-AOI
     distance 57.3px -> 37.5px over 158,891 samples; median vertical bias
     +8.5px -> +0.4px.
   - Fixed in: `fix/coordinate-space-loader` (get_trial_geometry,
     document_to_screenshot), `fix/wire-cursor-conversion` (the feature
     producer, per attribution flavor), and `approach-retreat@d5de80e` (replay
     bundles + the 147 published pages).
   - **crforager is unaffected**: T1 L1 moves 0.01 against a 0.20 gate.
     Leaky Cursor is NOT — final_dist/retreat_dist are the measurement.

2. **AOI card collisions.** Two cards measuring one DOM node. 508 duplicate
   cards across 454 trials, every one with the same signature (shift -1,
   adjacent order, verified true). Fixed with two-phase node claiming plus
   evidence-ranked awards: collisions 454 -> 0, orphaned main-column results
   480 -> 167 trials. Branch `fix/aoi-card-collision`.
   - Measured via coordinate collisions, independently of `css_path`, so this
     one is **not** affected by the harness bug below.

3. **Cellsplit trailing-drop.** The export is short on 902 of 1,551 carousels,
   median shortfall **exactly 1 cell**. Cells come from a frozen 2026-05-24
   snapshot with no producer in the repo. Not fixed. This is the worst-scoring
   layer (29.1% agreement) and the clearest next target.

### Confirmed NON-defects — do not "fix" these
- **The shift ladder in `measure_card_geometry` is a repair, not a bug.**
  `css_path` is unreliable: `_css_path()` builds nth-of-type indices from
  BeautifulSoup's parse and the browser's parse disagrees, so the selector lands
  on a neighbour on 5 of 12 cards in the trials checked. The ladder finds the
  right element by class+heading. Two attempts to "fix" it made things worse.
- **The y-DP alignment in `build_typed_aoi_map` pairs correctly.** Verified:
  AOI `y` is consistent with `a + s*geo_y` for its own card at 0.7px median on
  the trials that looked broken.
- **The builder already handles the y scale.** It fits it per trial by
  Theil-Sen and recovers `fit_slope` median 0.8996 against a true 0.9000 across
  2,774 trials. It is recorded in `build_typed_aoi_map_audits.jsonl`. Read that
  file before theorising about the alignment.

### Findings, not defects
- **Nobody pages the ad carousel.** It is a 3,796px scroller clipped to ~652px:
  27 cells in the DOM, ~5 visible. Across 1,582 carousels and 272 cell clicks,
  **zero** clicks landed on a card that was not already on screen — including in
  the 158 trials where the carousel WAS scrolled. Excluding those trials changes
  the CTR ordering not at all (rho -0.900 either way).
- **The published within-carousel rho = -1.0 is not supportable.** Positions 1
  and 2 are not ordered under any denominator tried. The direction holds; the
  perfect monotone does not. `paper.tex:156` needs restating in v4, not
  retracting. No replacement coefficient is established.

## The wrong turns, and what caught them

Four confident conclusions were wrong. Every one was caught by checking against
an artifact that already contained the answer, and every one was reached by
reasoning from a plausible model instead.

| claim | why it was wrong | what caught it |
|---|---|---|
| "The x correction is a translation, not a scale" | measured how the page re-lays-out at a narrower viewport; the capture SCALED a fixed layout | PNG dimensions vs the trial XML |
| "Carousel CTR rho = -0.90" | scored `vplaurlt`/`platop` ids that also appear in the right rail and double-count cells | the paper's own independently stated 1,582 carousels |
| "The typed map joins two coordinate systems uncorrected" | the builder fits the transform per trial and recovers it | `build_typed_aoi_map_audits.jsonl`, which records `fit_slope` |
| "The AOI failures are handle drift in the DP" | the harness resolved cards by an unreliable `css_path` | comparing the pipeline's own audit residuals (~1px) against the harness's verdict (catastrophic) |

The pattern is identical in all four: an internally coherent story, no external
check, and a cheap external check available the whole time.

**The strongest single heuristic from the day:** when two measurements disagree
violently, the prior should be that one of them is broken — not that the
disagreement is the finding.

## The fidelity harness (`scripts/aoi_fidelity.py`)

Built so claims about the substrate stop being arguments. Three scores against
DOM ground truth, deliberately separate because they fail independently:

| check | ground truth | status |
|---|---|---|
| `click` | the xpath each click event records | **trustworthy** |
| `aoi` | card identity (class + heading) vs the stored box, IoU | **rewritten today — re-baseline pending** |
| `cell` | numbered carousel ids inside the scroller's clip width | **trustworthy** |

Baseline on v1.1.0 as shipped: `click` 87.7%, `cell` 29.1%. **The first `aoi`
number (0.879 median / 87.0%) is void** — it resolved cards by `css_path` and so
measured the harness on the failing tail. The check now resolves by class+heading
identity, searching the document and scoring only unambiguous matches.

Critically, it does **not** reuse the pipeline's own resolution (css_path +
ladder + matchKind). If it did it would agree with the pipeline by construction
and could never detect a resolution error. Keep it that way.

## What I would do next

1. **Finish the `aoi` re-baseline** and only then judge `fix/aoi-card-collision`
   against it. Its own justification (508/508 duplicate signature) already
   stands independently.
2. **Cellsplit from the DOM.** 29.1% is the worst score, the trailing-drop is
   corpus-wide with a median shortfall of exactly 1, and the source is a frozen
   snapshot nothing in the repo can regenerate. The zero-paging finding settles
   the definitional question: visible cells are what participants experienced.
3. **Then re-derive**: Table 1, the 38,250 check, and an arXiv v4 change note.

## What I would not do

- Do not "fix" the shift ladder. It is load-bearing.
- Do not trust `css_path` for anything. Resolve by class+heading.
- Do not quote the `aoi` score until the re-baseline lands.
- Do not build a fix on a single worked example. Three of the four wrong turns
  above generalised from one trial that happened to fit.
